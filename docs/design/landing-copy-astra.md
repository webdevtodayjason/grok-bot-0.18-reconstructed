# 3. How it works

## 1. Pick a job to hand off.

Start with a computer task you repeat, such as checking the ticket queue each morning. Titanium helps set up your private assistant and connect the tools it needs.

## 2. Show your assistant how you do it.

Do the task once while your assistant watches a screen recording. It saves the steps so it can do the job on its own afterwards.

## 3. Give it a schedule, or ask in chat.

Have it run the task at set times, including overnight, or ask when you need it. Each reply includes a count of the tool results behind it, so you have evidence to check alongside its answer.

# 4. What it takes off your plate

These are examples of routines you can teach your assistant in your connected tools.

- **Tickets:** Check for tickets that have been open too long every two hours.
- **Email:** Answer a recurring customer question using the process you show it.
- **Reports:** Prepare a morning brief at 7:15 each weekday.
- **Follow-ups:** Send a follow-up email using the steps and timing you teach it.
- **Research:** Gather information from websites for a supplier comparison.
- **Marketing:** Draft a customer email about a service your business offers.

# 5. Why you can trust it

## Evidence with every reply.

Every reply shows a receipt count, such as “Backed by 7 tool results.” The system counts what the assistant actually ran, so the assistant cannot invent that count. Evidence of activity still needs your judgment about whether the work is right.

## Passwords stay out of chat.

When your assistant needs a password or an API key, it asks through a secure card. You paste it once into its locked store, without sending the value through the AI model or the chat.

## Your business gets its own private setup.

Your data stays on your own instance, hosted on Titanium's servers. It has its own storage, password, and address.

## Backups happen every night.

Your instance has nightly backups and a restore drill. Recovery is practiced, too.

## A person at Titanium is there to help.

Titanium Computing sells and supports your assistants. We work with you on setup, credentials, and monitoring.

# 7. FAQ

## Does it replace my staff?

It can take on recurring computer tasks you teach it. That is not the same as taking over someone's whole role. Start with a particular job your staff repeats, and judge the results before handing over more.

## What if it makes a mistake?

It can make mistakes. The receipt count shows evidence of what it ran, but does not guarantee that the result is correct. Check the work, especially while teaching a routine, and involve Titanium when you need help.

## Where is my data?

Your data stays on your private instance on Titanium's own servers, with its own storage, password, and address. You also choose the AI model it uses. Ask about your chosen model's data handling before giving it sensitive work.

## What does it cost to run?

The bring-your-own-keys plan has a monthly charge per instance, plus model usage costs based on your provider and workload. Prices are not set yet. Managed inference is coming, with pricing and included usage still to be set.

## What can it not do?

It works on a computer, using connected tools and tasks you teach it. It cannot do physical work, and you should not assume it can handle every task in every app. Talk through your specific job with Titanium before relying on it.

## How long does setup take?

We do not have a fixed setup time to quote. Titanium works with you on setup and credentials. Tell us which tools and first task you have in mind so we can discuss what is involved.

## Can I see what it did?

Every reply includes a receipt count computed from the tool results the system recorded. That gives you evidence behind the reply, rather than just the assistant's word that it worked. The count alone does not tell you whether the result is right.

## Can I stop it?

A specific stop or pause control is not confirmed here. Before scheduling work, ask Titanium to walk you through how a running task and future runs can be stopped. Do not assume that sending a chat message will stop a task already underway.
